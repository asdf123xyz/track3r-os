# OS-LOOPER — loop, overdub and save on the Record screen

Status: designed 2026-10-14 (maintainer asked for looper functions: overdub, count-in, play the recording and overdub), UI home from the UI map · **L1 merged 2026-10-16 (dev 3026c0f), its unit CPU measured (§3b); L2 merged 2026-10-17 (L2a 4d53e6b, L2b 07bf4f2, L2c f44acdd) and the L3 piece merged (6df27d7, dev 445df66): LOOPER is complete, L3 is not a separate slice (Q8); the by-ear checks are open; L2 amended for the Level C risk review in §3c (2026-10-16), which overrides §2 and §3b where they differ** · **Level C** (the render reads and writes one buffer while it plays) · **New Community OS behaviour** (our own design; no parity is claimed, and what the official recorder offers beyond recording is not claimed here). Depends on OS-METRO for the count-in (slice L3).

## 0. What exists (verified 2026-10-14)

- Record (`record_screen.h`, `recorder.h`): ALIGN (FREE / STEP / BAR / PATTERN) starts a take on the sequencer's timeline; STOP AT ends it, `N BARS` by itself after 16 N steps (OS-REC2); takes are written by the render into the sample arena's held free tail (`SampleBank::take_begin`); under REVIEW the pads play the take, TRIM START / END appear, F3 SAVE puts the window into the first free instrument slot, F4 DISCARD. Mono takes (our choice: a line input is summed to mono).
- The sample timeline is a `uint64` sample count (ADR 0013 D5); a bar is 16 steps (`recorder.h:86`).

## 1. Flow (one button, like a loop pedal)

```
LOOP = 4 BARS:  F1/Rec → [count-in, OS-METRO] → RECORDING 4 bars → LOOP REVIEW (plays in a loop, in time)
                F1/Rec = OVERDUB on / off · F5 UNDO LAYER · F8 LOOP ON / MUTED · F3 SAVE · F4 DISCARD
```

- **LOOP row** `OFF / 1..16 BARS` after STOP AT, on SOURCES, RESAMPLE and RADIO (one row: STOP AT's bar count with a loop flag). LOOP ≠ OFF makes the take start on a bar (ALIGN shows BAR, dimmed) and end after N bars (STOP AT shows LOOP, dimmed).
- The loop is **clocked to the bars**: it plays while the transport runs, from the bar the take began on, every N bars; Play stops and starts it with the sequencer (F8 is not a second transport). Armed with the transport stopped: the count-in starts the transport (OS-METRO §2); with COUNT-IN OFF the transport and the take start together.
- **LOOP REVIEW** replaces TRIM START / END with **FEEDBACK** (0..100 %, default 100: layers stay) and **LOOP VOL** (0..100, default 100). The header names the state: `REVIEW · LOOP 4 BARS` and `· OVERDUB` while overdubbing (UI map rule 7). The key row swaps as a whole.
- **SAVE** (F3): the loop becomes an instrument in the first free slot (the existing SAVE path), play mode LOOP FWD, exactly N bars of samples; the looper is free for the next layer. **DISCARD** (F4) throws the loop and its undo away.

## 2. Engine (Level C)

- **Loop player**: a source in the render that reads the loop buffer at `(t − t0) mod len` from the sample timeline (no drift), at LOOP VOL, into MAIN (and CUE as the strip routes it). It is **excluded from the RESAMPLE / REC taps** (a resample overdub must not record the loop into itself: the USB return's no-loopback rule, ADR 0012 amendment 6) and its routing is documented as an ADR 0013 amendment.
- **Overdub**: per sample `loop[p] = sat(loop[p] × FEEDBACK + in[p])`, the write head on the play head (§3c.4: the round-trip latency L left L2; every layer lands on layer 1 by construction, and the loop's lag against the bars is L1's and the plain recorder's, for a later alignment slice L4 with `diag rec latency`, not built yet). Single writer: only the render touches the buffer until SAVE; the pads' preview reads it during review (same render context, no race; §3c.4).
- **UNDO LAYER**: copy-on-write per 64-frame block: before the first write to a block in an overdub session, the block is copied to an undo buffer (a bitmap marks copied blocks); UNDO LAYER fades the loop out and puts them back block by block in the render, bounded by a measured cycle budget (§3c.6). One level.
- **Memory**: loop + undo buffer + bitmap as one take with a reserved tail in the sample bank (§3c.1): 2 × N bars + len/1024 B (4 bars at 120 BPM ≈ 8 s ≈ 2 × 705 KB of the arena). SAVE commits only the loop and trims the tail. State (positions, flags) outside the SRAM hot slab; the bitmap in the arena.
- **Tempo change** while a loop exists: the loop keeps its length in samples and restarts on each N-bar boundary; the header shows `TEMPO CHANGED`.
- CPU: the L1 read is 0.35 % kernel [unit]; the overdub, copy and restore are **to be measured** with bench kernels before they are wired, and gated on the worst block (§3c.7). Protocol: `docs/architecture/cpu-budget.md`; not merged until measured on the unit.

## 3a. L1 as built (2026-10-16) and the defaults it picked

Code: `audio/loop_player.h` (the player and its clock, the routing), `taps.h` (`RecTap::ended_on_boundary`), `graph.cpp` (`mix_extras`: the click and the loop, after the taps), `engine.cpp` (the Step and Stopped events), `app/recorder.h` (`cfg.loop_bars`, the loop take and review), `record_screen.cpp` / `record_actions.cpp` (rows, keys, header, SAVE). Routing: ADR 0013 amendment 4. Tests: `tests/audio_loop_test.cpp` (the clock against the real sequencer for ten minutes, stop and start, tempo changes, no self-recording through the real engine), `tests/record_screen_test.cpp` (the rows, the take, SAVE, DISCARD). Pictures: gallery states `record/loop_row`, `loop_review`, `loop_muted`.

- **Clock.** The loop is restarted by the sequencer's own Step events (the 16 N-th after it began, to the sample), and between restarts it is a sample counter: at a steady tempo that is `(t - t0) mod length`, but a free-running modulo would drift (the sequencer places a bar at a rounded sample, so N bars are not always the same number of samples; `audio_loop_test` measures the difference). A tempo change leaves the length in samples; the loop is cut short (faster) or silent at its end (slower) until the next restart.
- **Memory.** The loop is the take where it lies in the arena (no copy: nothing writes it in L1). L2's overdub brings its own working buffer and the undo buffer. The memory of a DISCARDed loop is the next take's, as for any discarded take (`take_begin` reuses it); `arm()` waits until the render has let go.
- **Defaults picked, to confirm by the maintainer** (everything below was not settled by the spec):
  1. **F1 before the N-th bar, a Stop during the take, or a full arena gives the loop take up** (a note says so); it does not become a shorter loop, because a loop that is not N bars cannot be clocked to the bars.
  2. **After a Stop the loop fades out over 32 frames (0.7 ms, across the block boundary when the stop is late in a block) and, when the transport starts again, resumes from its start on the first bar** (a start in the middle of a bar waits for the next one), ramped in over the same 32 frames (the first start after the take is ramped in too: about 0.7 ms of the downbeat's attack is missing in the first pass of the loop only); not from where it stopped. The loop's own wraps are dry cuts. A restart that comes a frame or two after the end of the take (a bar a sample longer at a fractional tempo) holds the take's last frame for 2 frames, so the loop never ticks; a longer gap (a slower tempo) is silence.
  3. **The loop goes to both channels of the line out (MAIN on the left jack and CUE on the right one in the split OUT MODE, both in Stereo) at LOOP VOL times the master level**, after the master chain and limiter (like the click and the USB return): the master volume scales it, the limiter does not see it, and it is not in the meters, scope, stems or any take.
  4. **LOOP is a row of its own, the last of each page** (after STOP AT), not a flag inside STOP AT; with LOOP on, ALIGN and STOP AT are drawn dim as BAR and LOOP and refuse edits with a message.
  5. **FEEDBACK is omitted in L1** (it joins LOOP VOL with L2); F1 under a loop's review is drawn dim (OVERDUB, L2) and refuses with "F4 DISCARDS THE LOOP: THEN F1 RECORDS ANOTHER"; F5 stays SEEK- (UNDO LAYER is L2).
  6. **LOOP VOL goes through the mixer level law** (100 is unity, 0.4 dB a step down to -40 dB), default 100; **F8 under a loop's review is the loop's mute switch** (LOOP ON / MUTED, a ramp over a block, the loop keeps its place on the bars); the pads still play the take.
  7. **SAVE saves the whole loop, no trim**, as instrument "LOOP nB" (play mode LOOP FWD over the whole sample), and the looper is then idle (the loop fades out).
  8. **Stopped, F1 with LOOP on starts the transport** (the Play key's pattern) with the take, after the count-in when COUNT-IN is on (the aligned take that waits for it already existed); with no transport attached it refuses.
  9. **The loader lets go first:** `SampleBank::clear()` and `new_project()` (a project load, NEW, a kit) call a hook that releases the loop and disarms a take before the arena is emptied (a fade of one block), and `OgSampleLoader::step` writes nothing until the render has let go (`SampleBank::clear_busy()`); the recorder then gives the take up with the note `TAKE LOST`. A loop that cannot fit the free arena at the tempo (N bars of `rate x 24 / tempo` frames) is refused at F1 (`NO ROOM IN THE SAMPLE MEMORY FOR THE LOOP`); one that outgrows it because the tempo slows under the take is given up with `THE SAMPLE MEMORY FILLED`; a loop that ends exactly on the last free frame is a loop.
  10. **Leaving the Record screen leaves the loop playing, like a loop pedal** (the recorder keeps its Review and the loop its phase on any screen; the Stop of the transport fades it out wherever the user is, the Play brings it back on the bar). F8, SAVE and DISCARD stay the Record screen's.
  11. The header reads `REVIEW - LOOP 4 BARS` (plain hyphen: the font has no middle dot), `RECORDING - LOOP ...`, and ` - TEMPO CHANGED` when the sequencer's tempo is not the one the loop was made at.
- **CPU, estimated:** per block with a loop playing, 64 frames of one SDRAM read, a multiply, two saturating adds and two stores, about 12 to 16 cycles a frame = 770 to 1,030 cycles of 261,120 = **0.3 to 0.4 %** [est]; nothing when no loop exists (one test in the render). The code is in flash (`O3OS_AUDIO_COLD`), SRAM_L grows by the call sites only. **Not merged until measured on the unit** (`diag cpu` with a loop playing, against the same song without).

## 3b. L2 and L3: plan against the measured L1 (2026-10-16)

Evidence marks: [unit] measured by the tester (bench image 2 `g248905f`, paired song windows; release v0.6.3, polled), [code] read from the L1 source, [est] estimate.

**L1 against its estimate.** §3a estimated 0.3 to 0.4 % [est]. [unit] the kernel is 925 cycles a block = **0.35 %** (it holds), but the song windows are higher: S1 (dense pattern) +0.74 % (about 1,950 cycles), S3 +0.34 %, loop present and stopped +0.28 % (about 730 cycles); polled on release v0.6.3 S1 +1.59 %, stopped +0.2 %. Two things the estimate left out: (1) the cache residual, S1 about 1,000 cycles beyond the kernel (the take's lines and the loop's code evict voice data; OS-CPUOPT1 §8.8, option L4 = eDMA prefetch of the take, not built); (2) the stopped cost: [code] `service()` + `serve()` run every block while a loop exists (3 atomic read-modify-writes, one `exchange`, then `run()` returns at its `gain_ == 0` test), about 100 cycles warm; the measured 520 to 730 is four to seven times that, which fits cold code lines of flash (28 cycles a line) and is not explained further [est]. The release figures are not comparable with the bench ones (a different build layout: OS-CPULAYOUT). The 0.5 % budget row therefore does not hold in a dense song (0.74 %); it holds in S3 (0.34 %).

**The spike of up to +4.6 % for 12 s after Stop is not the L1 tail** [code]: `on_stopped()` sets `running_ = false` and a 32-frame ramp to 0 (0.7 ms, inside one or two blocks); from the next block `gain_ == 0 && ramp_left_ == 0`, so `run()` returns before touching a sample, and nothing else in the player waits, counts or decays (`serve()` has no timer; the position does not advance when not running). 4.6 % is about 12,000 cycles, 13 times the kernel, so the loop cannot be what burns them even in principle. What lasts about 12 s after a Stop in the same render is the song's own tail: `voices_->release_all()` leaves sampler release envelopes, long samples and the delay / reverb sends ringing; the cost of those depends on where in the song the Stop fell. Most likely cause [est]: the paired windows stop at different song positions in the two arms, so the tails differ. **It is not proven**, and it costs no code to prove: stop the same song at the same row twice, once with the loop present and once with the loop released (F4), and read `diag bench stages` for the 12 s after the Stop, window by window. The loop's share sits in the output stage (`kStageConvert`: the click and the loop are timed there); a spike in the strips or render stages is the tail. If the loop-present arm is higher in the output stage by more than the 0.28 % residual, the cause is in `mix_extras` and this paragraph is wrong.

**L2 CPU: to be measured** (the figures this paragraph carried on 2026-10-16, 470 to 700 cycles steady, about 100 a copy-on-write, 65 a restored block, 1.0 s for a full restore, are **withdrawn** by the risk review: the measured L1 read kernel alone is 893 to 925 cycles a block [unit], about 14 cycles a frame, and a read-modify-write of the same frames cannot cost less; a 128 B SDRAM copy cannot cost 65 cycles). §3c.7 replaces them with bench kernels on the unit, run before the overdub is wired, and a gate on the worst block. What stays: nothing is added to the idle cost (the same `needs_service()` test); flash about +1.0 to 1.5 KB [est] (three kernels, the session, the restore, rows and keys); SRAM_L +0 or about +60 B depending on how the input reaches the kernel (§3c.4), against about 296 B free [docs]; bss +128 B if a staging block is needed, state about +64 B [est]; the undo buffer and its bitmap live in the arena (§3c.1). No persistent format changes: the loop and its undo are never written to a file, SAVE uses the existing instrument path.

**L3 is already built in L1.** §3a default 8 and `record_actions.cpp` (`record_toggle`): stopped, F1 with LOOP on starts the transport through `play_count_in` when COUNT-IN is on (1 or 2 bars; the take waits for the first bar after it) and through `play` otherwise; the test `loop screen: stopped, F1 starts the transport and the loop take together, after the count-in when COUNT-IN is on` covers both, and a loop take armed on a running transport shows the OS-METROALIGN countdown. What is left of L3 is the same gesture for the **overdub** (F1 under a loop's review while the transport is stopped), plus its QA rows. Cost: none in the render (the click and the sequencer already exist; OS-METRO's 0.2 % row). L3 shrinks to a small Level B change on top of L2 (recorder / action only, no audio code) if Q8 is accepted.

### 3c.1 The undo buffer must be held: a take with a reserved tail (finding 1, blocker; Q4; finding 10)

[code] `Recorder::finish()` ends a loop take with `w_.bank->take_end(frames)`; `SampleBank::take_end` sets `used_ = take_start + even(frames)` and clears `take_held`, so the arena after the loop is free for every other taker (`reserve` for a WAV, `alloc`, `edit_begin`, a slice table) while the loop plays, and `take_begin` rewinds a discarded take only when `used_ == take_start + even(take_frames)`. §3b's "the undo buffer follows the loop inside the same hold" was false as the bank stands: nothing holds it.

Rule: the bank gets a **take with a reserved tail**.
- `take_end(frames, tail_samples)` (a second parameter; 0 keeps the present behaviour for every other take): `used_ = take_start + even(frames) + even(tail)`; `take_tail = even(tail)` is stored in `SampleBankMem` (+4 B SDRAM). `take()` still reports the take alone; the tail begins at `take().pcm + even(frames)`.
- `take_begin` rewinds when `take_live && used_ == take_start + even(take_frames) + take_tail` (DISCARD gives both back, as long as nothing was allocated after the tail).
- `take_commit` trims the tail: when `used_ == take_start + even(take_frames) + take_tail`, `used_` becomes `take_start + even(take_frames)` and the tail is free again; otherwise (something was allocated after it while the loop was in review: a sample loaded from another screen, an edit buffer) the tail stays where it is, the price of the bump allocator, as for any uncommitted take. **Leaked-arena cost per saved loop** (finding 10): 0 in the common case; `len + bitmap` samples when something was allocated after the tail during the review (the screens' free-space figure shows it; a project load clears it). A counter is not worth its bytes; the test below proves both branches.
- `arm()`: the room `take_begin` gives is split as `room = 2 x cap + bitmap(cap)`, `bitmap(cap) = even(ceil(cap / 64 / 16))` samples (one bit per 64-frame block, 16 blocks per int16 sample); `rec().arm(pcm, cap, ...)` gets `cap`, not `room`, so a loop take that outgrows half the free arena overruns and is given up by the existing path (`overrun_frames() != 0`: `THE SAMPLE MEMORY FILLED`), never silently eats its undo. The refusal is `need > cap` (`NO ROOM IN THE SAMPLE MEMORY FOR THE LOOP`); `capacity_` is `cap`. **Boundary**: `need == cap` is accepted and a loop whose take ends exactly at `cap` is a loop; `need == cap + 1` is refused.
- `finish()` for a take that looped: `take_end(frames, frames + bitmap(frames))` (the undo buffer as long as the loop, the bitmap after it; both inside what `arm()` proved free, since `frames <= cap`). A loop take that did not loop keeps `take_end(0)`.

**As built (L2a, 2026-10-17).** The split uses the even-rounded sizes the bank really reserves: `cap` is the largest `c` with `2 x even(c) + bitmap(c) <= room` (`SampleBank::loop_capacity`, `undo_bitmap_samples`), so `take_end(c, c + bitmap(c))` never meets the arena's end (for an odd `c` the spec's `2 c + bitmap` is two samples short). `take_tail` is cleared by `take_begin`, `clear()` and `new_project()`. Leaked arena per saved loop: 0 samples when nothing is allocated after the tail during the review, `frames + bitmap(frames)` samples (about 2 x the loop: 64,032 for the 2-bar test loop) otherwise; DISCARD frees lazily (the rewind happens at the next `take_begin`, so the screens' free-space figure shows the take until then, as for any discarded take). Cost: flash +344 B, SRAM and SDRAM_STATIC +0 (the tail field is 4 B in `SampleBankMem`). Known edge, not fixed here (render code, for L2c): `RecTap::capture` ends a take by capacity (`done + take >= capacity_`) in the block that fills it, so a loop take of exactly `cap` frames whose end step falls at the next block's first sample is cut (`LoopCut`) instead of looping; any loop shorter than `cap` is unaffected.

Tests (`tests/sample_bank_test.cpp`): the tail held against `reserve`, `alloc`, `edit_begin` and a slice table (they land after the tail or refuse); rewind on DISCARD with and without an allocation after the tail; trim on commit with nothing after (`used_` shrinks) and with something after (`used_` unchanged, the tail leaked, the take committed); `take_end(frames, 0)` unchanged; the `arm()` boundary at `need == cap` and `cap + 1` (`tests/record_screen_test.cpp`). Non-loop takes are untouched.

### 3c.2 SAVE is two steps, and the writer never writes after Idle (finding 2, blocker)

[code] `Recorder::commit()` calls `take_commit` (the slot adopts the memory and a rebind is requested) and then `loop().release()`; the render takes the release in `serve()` at its next block, fades over up to 32 frames and calls `drop()` at the end of that block. For L1 this is a read after the adoption and harmless; for L2 an overdub would write a slot's audio for one more block. And `drop()` publishes Idle at the end of `serve()`: a read-modify-write placed after `serve()` in `service()` would run after Idle.

Rules:
- **Order inside the render.** The RMW (3c.4) runs inside `serve()`, after the request exchange and after `run()` has mixed the block (3c.4 says why), and **not at all** in a block where the request was `kRelease` or `dropping` is set, where the state is not `Loop`, where the render is parked, or where the overdub flag read at the block's start is off. `drop()` stays the last thing. No write after Idle, by construction; the sentinel test of 3c.8 proves it.
- **SAVE (F3) with a session on.** Step 1: the recorder posts **quiesce** (a request that ends the session with its punch-out ramp) and goes to `SaveWait`; the render acks by publishing `writing()` false (a new atomic, render-written: set at the first write of a session or restore step, cleared by the punch-out block, the restore's end and `drop()`). Step 2: the next `poll()` that reads `writing()` false runs the present `commit()` (`take_commit`, then `release()`). The wait is one or two blocks (1.5 to 3 ms, under one app frame): **the screen keeps drawing the review; a second F3, F1, F4 or F5 during `SaveWait` is ignored**, no message (the user cannot see the window). With no session on and no restore running, SAVE is the present single step (`writing()` is already false).
- **SAVE during a restore** is **refused** with Info `UNDO IN PROGRESS` (decided by the spec session for the maintainer, 2026-10-16: an aborted restore would leave a half-restored loop; a restore lasts about a second, 3c.6).
- **DISCARD (F4)** with a session on or a restore running: `release()` as today; the render's release path skips the RMW and the restore step and drops at the end of the block. `arm()` already waits for Idle [code: `Recorder::arm` returns `Busy` while `phase() != Idle`].
- The loader hook (`on_bank_clear` -> `release()`, `bank_clear_busy` -> `phase() != Idle`) needs no change: the same path, no write after Idle.

**As built (L2c, 2026-10-17).** The acknowledgement is a **sequence number**, not the `writing()` level: `LoopPlayer::overdub_on() / quiesce() / undo()` each post one command word (`seq << 4 | kind`, one atomic, the latest replaces an untaken one, so a quiesce beats an overdub-on that was not yet taken) and return `seq`; the render publishes `ack = seq` after the flags the command caused (`overdubbing()`, `restoring()`, `layer()`, one flags byte) and, for a command that ends a session, only once the punch-out ramp is done and nothing more will be written (`acked(seq)`). `Recorder::begin_save()` posts quiesce and sets `save_wait_`; `App::poll_recorder` runs `record.save` again when `save_ready()` (an ack of that sequence) and cancels the wait if the action refuses or the screen was left. F3, F1, F4 and F5 are ignored without a message while `save_waiting()`. The RMW is `LoopPlayer::run()` (the play step plus the write of the frames it just played), inside `serve()`, never in a block that releases (`dropping` clears the session before the segments run), never when parked or muted; the sentinel test (`audio_loop_test`: SAVE and DISCARD during a session and during a restore, four variants) compares loop, undo buffer and bitmap bit for bit before the release, at Idle, and eight blocks later with pending commands. **The exactly-`cap` loop**: `RecTap::push` ends a take by capacity only when it has no step count (`auto_steps_`) or when frames did not fit (`take < limit`); a loop take that fills the memory exactly waits for its end step, which may be the first sample of the next block (test: `a take of exactly the capacity is a loop`; it fails without the fix).

**As built (L2c, 2026-10-17), what differs from the table.** (1) F5 with no layer, during a restore or after one says `NO LAYER TO UNDO`; F1 and F3 during a restore (or while the UNDO command is not yet acknowledged) say `UNDO IN PROGRESS`; F4 and F8 are accepted. (2) UNDO is one command: the session ends (punch-out), then `start_restore()` fades the loop out and the restore steps run once it is silent, from the next block. (3) The restore keeps the loop's clock: `running_` and the step count go on, the loop is silent (`restoring_`, then `resume_`) and fades in at its **next restart** (the loop's own N-bar boundary, so the loop is back on the bars it was on); a transport stopped meanwhile resumes by the usual first-bar rule. (4) Stop keeps the session: the write gain ramps out over `kFadeFrames` with the play head (`wsync`), nothing is written while stopped, and it ramps in with the restart; a muted (silent) render writes nothing and ramps in when the sound is back. (5) `park(true)` clears the session, the restore, the pending command and the resume flag, publishes the flags (`layer()` stays) and acknowledges every command; the layer so far is still undone by UNDO after the export. (6) The bitmap is cleared by `Recorder::overdub_toggle()` before overdub-on is posted, refused while `overdubbing()`, `undo_busy()` or an unacknowledged command (a punch-out in flight). (8) The input stays owned while `ov_wanted_ || overdubbing()`, on any screen. (9) The overview is rebuilt, a bounded 65,536 frames a poll (`kRefillBudget`), when a session or a restore ends.

### 3c.4 The write head, the latency and the input (findings 5, 7)

[code] `taps.h:237`: a take of an external source arrives one RX slot late and nothing compensates it, so **take 1 of a line / mic / radio loop already lags the bars by the round trip** (about 4.4 ms [est]); a RESAMPLE take reads MAIN and has no lag. §2 and §3b placed the layers at `pos - L` with one constant L = 194: right only for the analogue sources, and even there the layers would land L **ahead of layer 1**, which is what the ear judges in an overdub.

Decision (decided by the spec session for the maintainer, 2026-10-16): **L leaves L2.** The write head is the play head: `loop[pos] = sat(loop[pos] x FB + in[pos])`, where `in` is this block's input as the tap would record it (the REC bus mixed to mono or one channel, or MAIN for RESAMPLE). Every layer then lands exactly on layer 1 for every source, by construction: both carry the same lag, whatever it is, and the RESAMPLE case (lag 0) is right too. What L2 does not fix is the lag of the whole loop against the bars for an external source: that is the L1 (and the plain recorder's) state, unchanged, and it becomes a small later slice **L4, loop alignment**: measure L per source with `diag rec latency` (not built yet [code: no such command]; a cable from line out to line in, a click, the offset in frames), then rotate the read head (`read at (pos + L) mod len`, one split of the read kernel at the wrap) and rotate the memory once at SAVE. Nothing of L2 has to change for L4. With no L there is no "first L frames after Play" to discard (finding 5): the input after a restart lands at the loop's start, where layer 1 holds the input of the same instant.

- **Mix, then write** (decided): `run()` adds the loop as it was, then the RMW rewrites the same frames; the new layer is heard from the next pass, not twice in this one (the player hears themselves through MONITOR already; a loop pedal does the same). The pads' preview of the take ([code] `record_actions.cpp:225`, `record_pad`: a preview descriptor over the take's memory, so **the voices do read the take during review**, correcting §2) hears the layer as it is written: same render context, no race, acceptable.
- **The input.** (As built L2c: the REC bus planes are intact after `run_taps`, so `Graph::mix_extras` reads them in place: the left or right plane for those channels, `kernel::mono_mix` into the graph's `mono_` scratch for Mix, only while `LoopPlayer::wants_input()`; no staging block, SRAM_L +0 B.) Preferred: the RMW reads the REC bus (or MAIN for RESAMPLE) in place from `mix_extras()`, if the engineer confirms in `graph.cpp` that the bus block is intact after the taps (no staging, no SRAM_L); otherwise a 64-frame mono staging block (`alignas(4)`, bss +128 B) filled by a hook in `RecTap::push` (about +60 B of SRAM_L; `.ramfunc_l` had 312 B free before OS-CPUOPT1, which merged at +16 B [docs: STATUS 2026-10-16], so about 296 B: the engineer records the real figure from `tools/ci.sh mk66`). The channel and source are the loop's (Q9); the mono law is the tap's (`(l + r) / 2`, or one channel).
- **The RMW as a standalone function** (finding 3), not a lambda (the L1 lambda was not inlined at -Os: OS-CPUOPT1): `loop_rmw(int16_t* loop, const int16_t* in, uint32_t n, int32_t fb)` (FEEDBACK is Q16, 65536 = 100 %: see 3c.7 as built) in `audio/src/loop_player.cpp`, in flash; a variant without multiplies for FB = 100. By words where aligned: a leading odd halfword when `pos` is odd, the word body (`ldr` loop, `ldr` input; with FB < 100 two `smulwb` / `smulwt`, packed with `pkhbt` before one `qadd16`), a trailing odd halfword at an odd `len` or block end; saturation at -32768 / 32767 by `qadd16`. The host test checks it against a scalar reference at both start parities and odd lengths.
- **Copy-on-write**: before the first RMW of a 64-frame loop block in a session (bitmap bit clear), `loop_cow_copy(undo + b x 64, loop + b x 64, n)` copies it (n < 64 for the last partial block of a `len` that is not a multiple of 64) and sets the bit. A render block's write span starts at any `pos` and is cut at `len_` and at a restart, so it touches **at most 4 bitmap blocks** (two before a restart, two after); that bounds the copies per render block.
- **FEEDBACK** (Q7): applied only to frames the write head passes while the session is on; at FB = 0 the layer replaces, at 100 it adds. Q16 gain (65536 = 100 %), no `double`.
- `ov_` (the take's overview) is stale after a session: the recorder rebuilds it from the main loop when `overdubbing()` is acked off and when a restore ends (`view_.build()` reads at most 4,096 samples; the render is read-only then).

### 3c.5 The input is owned while the session is on (finding 6)

[code] `Recorder::apply`: `own = in_view_ || Armed || Recording`; Review does not own, so leaving the Record screen with a loop in review gives the engine's input path back to the Mixer's board, and an overdub on another screen would record whatever the board routes to REC (often silence), and FEEDBACK below 100 would decay the loop. Decision (decided by the spec session for the maintainer, 2026-10-16): **`own` also while the session is on** (`|| overdub_on_`): the pedal keeps its input on any screen (§3a default 10 lets the loop play anywhere; overdubbing a RESAMPLE while editing the pattern is the point). The input goes back to the board when the session ends off-screen. One term in `apply()`; a test in `record_screen_test`: leave the screen with the session on, the plan stays applied; F1 off on return gives it back.

### 3c.6 UNDO LAYER: the restore, bounded by cycles (Q3, finding 3)

The loop fades out (32 frames), then each render block copies dirty blocks back from the undo buffer (`loop_restore_step`, standalone, flash) until **a cycle budget `kRestoreCycles` is spent or the bitmap is exhausted**; then the restore ends, `layer_` and `writing()` clear, and the loop fades in on its next bar. `kRestoreCycles` is set from the bench (3c.7), not guessed: §3b's "65 cycles for 128 B" was wrong (an SDRAM read and write of 128 B cannot cost less than the L1 read kernel's about 14 cycles a frame [unit]). **DWT guard**: the step reads the graph's cycle counter (`Graph::cycles_`, DWT CYCCNT, the one `diag cpu` samples) at its start and **skips this block** when the block has already used more than 70 % of its 261,120 cycles (a heavy block of voices: the restore takes a block longer rather than risking the deadline). The header shows `- UNDO` while the restore runs (decided). Duration: `ceil(dirty / blocks_per_step)` render blocks when no block is skipped; the spec sets no figure until the bench gives one (§3b's "1.0 s" is withdrawn).

**Forced step (L2b review, finding 1, MAJOR).** The load guard may skip a block's step, but **never more than `kRestoreForceAfter` (8) blocks in a row**: the ninth block does its step whatever its load, so the restore always ends and a loaded song cannot starve it. A step is bounded three ways: `kRestoreStepCopies` (4) blocks copied (`loop_restore_step` is called with one copy at a time), `kRestoreStepWords` (32) bitmap halfwords read in total, and `kRestoreCycles` (6,000, **confirmed on the unit, 2026-10-17: `diag bench looper` restore_step avg 2.81k, max 4.34k cycles, about a 38 % margin [unit]**) spent after the first copy; the forced step obeys the same bounds. A budget of 0 passed to the kernel counts as 1 (the step always progresses). The bitmap is never tested for zero to find the end (a stray bit above the last block of its last halfword is never cleared): the end is the cursor reaching `ceil(len / 64)`, and the session only sets bits of blocks below it. Tests: `audio_loop_test` (the restore is bounded: with a load above 70 %, never more than `kRestoreForceAfter + 2` blocks in a row without progress, 4 copies at most a block, and the restore ends).
