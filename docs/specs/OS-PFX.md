# OS-PFX: Chord 14, Arp 21, Tune 40 and Slide 41/42 act

Level C (the sequencer tick and the sampler voices are real-time paths) · release-note items PAR-038, PAR-078, PAR-046, PAR-079 (documented, not verified; Glide, Overdrive and Bit depth are OS-IFX1) · **no persistent format change**: the ids and values were stored already, and `fx_sanitize` still clamps a value at the point of use, never in the file (PAR-003 DEC-1).
Tags: **V** verified parity (evidence cited), **N** new Community OS behaviour (documented, one table or constant to reverse), **Q** open question (the OG's behaviour is not known; nothing is invented; an open research question). Evidence in this file is a section of the official manual that is named, or a measurement on an official unit that is recorded in this repository (none is, so far). A test pins what this OS does and is not evidence about the official firmware. A PAR number is the label of a row in the maintainer's parity ledger, which is not in this repository, and "contract" was the pre-release note that listed the arp rules (also not here): neither is evidence on its own, so what only they backed is Q.

## The FX (all are read when a note STARTS, except Slide, which the voice follows)
- **Chord 14, stored 0..29.** **Q** (OS-PFXTAB is the work item that put this table in; this repository holds no record of a measurement on an official unit, so whether the table matches the official firmware's chord map is an open research question; the tests pin what this OS does): the byte indexes a table of 30 semitone masks (`seq/fx.h kChordMask`); value 0 is no chord, 1..29 are chords of two to four tones, root first and ascending (bit b of the mask is the step's note + b semitones, b = 0..14): 1 `27` (0 2 7, Sus2), 2 `28`, 3 `36`, 4 `37` (Min), 5 `47` (Maj), 6 `48`, 7 `57` (Sus4), 8 `5A`, 9 `5C` (Open4), 10 `67`, 11 `7C` (Open5), 12 `7E`, 13 `279`, 14 `27A`, 15 `27B`, 16 `369`, 17 `36A`, 18 `378`, 19 `379` (Min6), 20 `37A` (Min7), 21 `37B`, 22 `479` (Maj6), 23 `47A` (Maj7), 24 `47B` (MajMaj7), 25 `489`, 26 `48A`, 27 `48B`, 28 `57A`, 29 `57B`; the label is one hex digit per offset above the root (`37A` is 3, 7, 10). Eleven of the 29 chords carry a name (`app/fx_text.h chord_name`); the info line gives the name where there is one, and always the interval label (`CHORD MIN7 37A`, `CHORD 5A`). A note past 127 is dropped from the chord (Q: whether the official firmware skips it too is not recorded). Fixtures and projects store the byte: **no format change** (PFX is unreleased); a value past 29 is held at 29 by `fx_sanitize` where it is read. Open (Q): whether the official firmware's chord map is this one, whether earlier versions of it used the same order, names for the other 18, what a sampler does with a Chord on the official firmware (here: root only, maintainer decision below).
- **Arp 21, 0..33.** **Q** (OS-PFXTAB is the work item that put this map in; no record of a measurement on an official unit is in this repository, so whether the map matches the official firmware's is an open research question; the tests pin what this OS does): 0 is off, `v - 1 = 11 x direction + rate`, direction 0 up `/`, 1 down `\`, 2 random `R`; the rate is the period between two notes in microsteps, 48 to a step: **384, 288, 192, 144, 96, 48** (8, 6, 4, 3, 2, 1 steps) and **24, 16, 12, 8, 6** (1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/6, 1/8 of a step). Rules (**Q**, as above: parity of each rule with the official firmware is an open research question; the tests pin what this OS does): the arp needs a **Chord FX in the other column** (without one it does nothing); a **Chord of value 0 gives the chromatic arpeggio** over the twelve semitones above the note (so Chord 0 + Arp 6 plays C, C#, D ... B, a step each); the arp visits the offsets **0..11 only**, never 12 or 14 (chord 9 `5C` arps as 0 5, chords 11 `7C` and 12 `7E` as 0 7, while the chord alone sounds all its tones); **the walk (OS-PFXFIX, Q as above):** the arp keeps a position 0..11 (`arp_pos`) where the next search starts; up searches upward from it (wrapping at 12) for the first offset of the mask, plays it and sets the position to that offset + 1 (start 0, so the root first); down searches downward, sets offset - 1 (start 11, so the highest tone below 12 first); random starts the upward search at the position plus a random 0..10 (wrapping at 12) and sets offset + 1 (start 0) - so it is **not uniform over the tones** (for the tones 0 and 7 the root is 5/12 of the notes, not 1/2; a chromatic random arp never repeats the note it has just played). The first note is picked the same way at setup and replaces the step's note. The note is **`min(base + offset, 127)`**: a clamp, no tone is lost near the top (the CHORD, MIDI block, still skips the tones of 128 and up). **N:** the RNG source (`seq::Rng`, xorshift32 seeded at every transport start; the random sequence is this OS's; whether the official firmware's distribution has the structure described above is Q); what stops an arp is this OS's; on a sample the arp retriggers (Q5).
- **Tune 40, stored 0..48.** The scale -24..+24 semitones, so 24 is no change, is documented in the release notes (label PAR-046; not verified, not a verified-parity claim). **N:** the note is shifted by `value - 24`, held to 0..127, before it is sent. **Q:** the official firmware's meaning on a step without a note (a held voice retuned) is not known: **N** such a step ignores it.
- **Slide up 41 / down 42, 0..255.** Documented in the official manual §7.36-7.37 (documented behaviour, not verified parity, OS-MANUAL2 §5; label PAR-079): sixteenths of a semitone from the step note, 16 is one semitone. **Q: how long it takes. N:** the pitch of the sampler voice moves linearly to the distance in ONE STEP of the tempo in force and stays there; a note that starts under it begins at once, a step without a note slides the sounding voice from where it is to the distance from the note, the FX ending (or the next note step without it) gives the note's own pitch back. Evaluated once a block (the pitch is a staircase of at most one block), through the control table's Slide group as before; the voice side is `voices.h SLIDE FX`.

## Tick timing
Chord: the extra MIDI notes start on the note's own sample, root first, and end with it (Gate, the next note, Off, Cut, Fade, Roll repeat and stop all end every one). Arp: note n starts exactly `period x n` microsteps after the first, counted from the note's start (Micro-move included); a note ends as the next starts; it runs while the step is open (until the next note, Off, Cut, Fade or 9999 microsteps) and wins over a Roll started later. Tune: at the note's sample. Slide: with the note (the ModSet comes before the NoteOn), then per block.

## Same step, same track
First column wins for each of the three; Chord and Arp use both columns together, so Gate, Roll, Chance and the like share a step with a Chord alone. Random note and Tune act before the chord is built (the whole chord moves); Velocity and Gate apply to every note; a Roll repeats the chord. Chance cancels all. A note of an empty instrument slot makes no sound, so it sets up no chord and no arp (an arp would otherwise play the notes on the instrument of an earlier step). On a step without a note the three do nothing and leave the column's voice FX alone. A Slide on a MIDI instrument's step is ignored.

| instrument | Chord | Arp | Tune | Slide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MIDI out | all notes together (one velocity, one channel) | one note at a time (note-off then on) | the MIDI note shifted (**Q7**: N here) | ignored (needs pitch bend: OS-MIDI2) |
| sample, Modern engine | root only (one voice a track) | retriggers the voice | note shifted | pitch ramp |
| beat slice | root only | retriggers (a slice a note) | ignored (the note picks the slice) | pitch ramp |
| Amiga engine, SD module | root only | retriggers | note shifted | ignored (no pitch input) |

## UI (ui-map §4, §5)
The five join the FX list (appended after Gate Length: picker 36..40, every earlier index keeps its place; the popup scrolls, 41 entries) and the info line says the FX under the cursor in words (Chord 20 `CHORD MIN7 37A`, Chord 8 `CHORD 5A`, Arp 14 `ARP DOWN 4 STEPS`, Arp 7 `ARP UP 1/2 STEP`, Tune 31 `TUNE +7 ST`, Slide up 35 `SLIDE UP 2.19 ST`; `app/fx_text.h`; a value past the range reads as the range's end, as the sequencer reads it, so Chord 200 says `CHORD 57B`). No key, page or row is added.

## Real time, cost, tests
No allocation, no double, bounded loops (a chord is at most 4 notes, a chromatic arp up to 12 (`arp_notes[12]`, 8 B more per track), `step()` still bounded by the microsteps in the block); the per-track state is `TrackState` +2 bytes and a 15-byte `PfxState`, apart so that the loop every track walks every microstep stays small. A row with none of the three pays a few compares. The voice adds three words to `Voice`, a flag test in `mod_block` (flash) and no hot code. Host tests: `seq_pfx_test.cpp` (every chord, every arp rate, timing to the sample, edges, combinations, a MIDI hang fuzz, block-size determinism), `audio_voices_test.cpp` (the pitch law, the ramp, the engine route), the table tests, the info line's words against the sequencer's reading for every value 0..255 of the four FX (`pattern_edit_rules_test.cpp`), and the router's cases of a full staging queue (`midi_router_test.cpp`). Unit check (`diag cpu`): the same dense pattern, 12 tracks of notes on every step, once without the FX and once with Chord+Arp on the MIDI tracks and Tune+Slide on the sample tracks; then, because the TB-303 song is the heaviest load the unit carries, the same again on a track of the built-in TB-303 module, plain and with Chord 1 + Arp 11 (16 module notes a step, each a retrigger) (`qa_pattern.json`, `CPU`).

**Measured cost** (mk66 image from the ELF, `arm-none-eabi-size -A`, 0.4.11 against this branch): flash +3,480 B (image 578,368 to 581,848), SRAM_U `.bss` +272 B (129,936 to 130,208), SRAM_L -16 B (`.ramfunc_l` 27,364 to 27,348; `.tapmem_l` unchanged). The state behind the +272 is larger than it looks: `g_seq_storage` +272 B (`PfxState` 16 x 15 = 240, `TrackState` +32) and `g_voices` +224 B (`Voice` +16 B each x 12 voices = 192: three new 4-byte fields, padded to the struct's alignment; `slide_fx_` 32 B; `slide_on_` 2 B, which sits in padding), 496 B in all; the 224 B of the voices were absorbed by alignment padding that was in the layout, so a later growth of the same neighbours pays in full.

**OS-PFXFIX:** `PfxState` loses `arp_n` and `arp_idx` for the position byte `arp_pos` (the struct stays 14 B: a pad byte takes the freed one). Cost (mk66 ELF, 0.4.14 against this branch): flash `.text` +16 B (601,520 to 601,536), SRAM_U `.bss` +0 (197,576).

**OS-PFXTAB cost** (mk66 image from the ELF, 0.4.13 against this branch): flash `.text` +288 B (563,776 to 564,064), SRAM_U `.bss` +16 B (130,792 to 130,808: `arp_period` is 16 bit because the slowest arp period is 384 microsteps; the arp keeps its tones as a mask and a base note, not a note list, so the 12-tone chromatic arp costs no array).

**MIDI traffic (new with Chord and Arp).** The DIN out sends no running status: a note-on or a note-off is 3 bytes, 960 us at 31,250 baud, so at most about 1,040 messages a second (3.1 kB/s). A chord sends four times the notes of a plain step (four note-ons and four note-offs); an Arp note is an off and an on, so Arp at the fastest rate, 6 microsteps (1/8 of a step, value 11, 22 or 33; OS-PFXTAB: the arp grid ends at 6; the Roll grid goes to 3) is 64 notes a second a track at 120 BPM, 384 B/s, and **four such tracks are 1.54 kB/s at 120 BPM and exceed the DIN above about 240 BPM**. Past that the DIN falls behind: the notes arrive late and, when the 256-item staging queue of `MidiOut` fills, are lost (`Stats::staging_overflow`, not on a console line yet); since this fix the hang book keeps a lost note-off owed, so the next stop, mute or panic still releases the note. USB MIDI is not limited this way. The sequencer does not limit anything: a pattern that asks for more than the DIN carries is the player's to thin out.

## Open questions and choices (reversible)
Q1 chord value map and Q2 arp value map: **settled by OS-PFXTAB as this OS's tables** (parity with the official firmware's maps: Q), Q3 slide duration (`VoicePool::slide_start`: one step), Q4 Tune on a step without a note, Q5 chord and arp on non-MIDI instruments (root only / retrigger: chosen so every instrument does something audible), Q6 Slide on MIDI and on Amiga/module voices (ignored), **Q7 Tune on a MIDI instrument**: PAR-084 (1.4.0) says Tune in *Performance* mode also affects MIDI notes (documented in the release notes, not verified), so a Pattern step's Tune shifting a MIDI note is this OS's choice (N); what the official firmware does for a Pattern step is an open research question. The official firmware's behaviour on any of these needs a measurement on an official unit, recorded here, before parity is claimed.
